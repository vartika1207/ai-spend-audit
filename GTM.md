# GTM

The exact target user is a technical founder, CTO, or engineering manager at a seed to Series B startup with 5–80 employees and a fast-growing AI-tool stack. They likely adopted Cursor, ChatGPT Team, Claude Team, Copilot, and direct API usage organically, without a formal procurement process. They feel the spend is annoying but not yet big enough to justify a finance project.

Right before they want this tool, they are probably searching or scrolling through topics like “Cursor pricing too expensive,” “ChatGPT Team vs Plus,” “Claude Team worth it,” “OpenAI API bill high,” or “AI tools for engineering teams.” They also notice the problem during monthly finance review, runway planning, or when an engineer asks for another paid AI seat.

They hang out on X founder circles, Indie Hackers, Hacker News, r/SaaS, r/startups, r/LocalLLaMA, r/ChatGPTCoding, AI Engineer Discord, Latent Space communities, and startup Slack groups. The most specific wedge is not broad SEO. It is showing founders a screenshot-friendly savings result and asking them to compare their own stack.

To get the first 100 users in 30 days with $0 paid budget, I would start with 30 direct DMs to founders and engineering leads using a specific hook: “I built a free AI spend audit. It flags wasted Cursor/ChatGPT/Claude seats in 2 minutes. Want me to run it on your public stack assumptions?” Then I would post three teardown threads on X: one for a 5-person startup, one for a 20-person dev team, and one for API-heavy usage. I would submit a useful non-sales post to Hacker News: “I built a tiny calculator for AI tool overspend.” I would also ask interview users to share their anonymized result link.

The unfair distribution channel for Credex is that it already knows where real discounted AI credits exist. Most calculators can say “spend less”; Credex can say “here is a path to buy equivalent credits cheaper.” Week-1 traction would look like 100 completed audits, 25 saved reports, 8 high-savings consultation requests, and at least 3 conversations with teams spending over $1,000/month.
